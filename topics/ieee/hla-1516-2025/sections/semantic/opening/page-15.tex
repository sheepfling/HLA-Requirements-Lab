\HLASubclauseHeading[10.02pt]{clause-1.4.5}{1.4.5 Rules}
\vspace{12.10pt}

The HLA rules complete the framework. They require the use of the OMT and Federate Interface\linebreak
specifications as well as sound development principles and practices to ensure the proper operation of a\linebreak
federation execution.\par
\vspace{20.40pt}

\HLASubclauseHeading[10.02pt]{clause-1.4.6}{1.4.6 Implementation independence}
\vspace{12.16pt}

The HLA allows distributed simulation systems to be developed and executed for a range of purposes in a\linebreak
standardized manner. The HLA does not prescribe a specific implementation, nor does it mandate the use\linebreak
of any particular programming language or software. Over time, as technology advances become available,\linebreak
new and different implementations will be possible within the framework of the HLA.\par
\vspace{23.04pt}

\HLASubclauseHeading{clause-1.5}{1.5 Relationship between HLA and distributed simulation engineering processes}
\vspace{12.62pt}

The design, development, integration, and testing of distributed simulation environments such as HLA\linebreak
federations is fundamentally a systems engineering discipline. Two recommended practices have been\linebreak
developed to provide systems engineering guidance specifically tailored to distributed simulation\linebreak
environments, the Distributed Engineering and Execution Process (DSEEP) and the DSEEP Multi-\linebreak
Architecture Overlay (DMAO). The DSEEP provides guidance for engineering single-architecture\linebreak
distributed simulation environments. The DMAO provides extended guidance on issues and solutions\linebreak
unique to distributed simulation environments employing more than one simulation architecture.\par
\vspace{22.56pt}
