\HLARationaleHeading{rationale-4}{A.1.4 Rationale for Rule 4}
\vspace{12.25pt}

\HLANormativeRuleStatement{During a federation execution, joined federates shall interact with the RTI in accordance with the HLA\linebreak interface specification.}
\vspace{11.87pt}

By requiring a standardized, common interface between federates and the RTI, along with a common\linebreak
application programmer’s interface (API), the HLA allows for independent development and\linebreak
implementation of federate applications. Federate developers can work independently and develop\linebreak
interfaces to the RTI without regard to RTI implementation, and RTI developments can proceed without\linebreak
explicit consideration of federate development. The separation of the interfaces from the requirements for\linebreak
federate data exchange allows for the reuse of a common interface specification across the broad spectrum\linebreak
of distributed M\&S applications, with specific application needs tailored through the FOM mechanism.\par
\vspace{23.42pt}

\HLARationaleHeading{rationale-5}{A.1.5 Rationale for Rule 5}
\vspace{12.25pt}

\HLANormativeRuleStatement{During a federation execution, an instance attribute shall be owned by at most one joined federate at any\linebreak given time.}
\vspace{11.81pt}

The HLA also provides the mechanism during federation execution to dynamically transfer ownership of\linebreak
an instance attribute from one joined federate to another or for a joined federate to unconditionally divest\linebreak
its ownership of an instance attribute. In this second case, the instance attribute becomes “unowned” by all\linebreak
joined federates, hence the rule explicitly states that “an instance attribute shall be owned by at most one\linebreak
joined federate at any given time.” Joined federates can request ownership of “unowned” instance\linebreak
attributes. By defining ownership at the instance attribute level and providing the tools to hand off\linebreak
ownership during federation execution, the HLA provides a flexible tool set for using various combinations\linebreak
of joined federates to meet user needs.\par
\vspace{21.64pt}

\HLAClauseHeading[10.98pt]{clause-A.2}{A.2 Federate rules}
\vspace{24.09pt}

\HLARationaleHeading{rationale-6}{A.2.1 Rationale for Rule 6}
\vspace{12.31pt}

\HLANormativeRuleStatement{Federates shall have an HLA SOM, documented in accordance with the HLA OMT.}
\vspace{11.80pt}

A major goal of the HLA is to support interoperability and reuse of simulations. The HLA provides this\linebreak
support by providing for reuse at the level of simulations (or, more generally, federates) and allowing\linebreak
access to the representations in those simulations.\par
\vspace{11.98pt}

Lack of cost-effective access to information about the object representations available in federates inhibits\linebreak
reuse. The requirement for a SOM addresses information access by requiring federates to document the\linebreak
minimum essential, salient aspects of their capabilities to allow for easy identification of the federates’\linebreak
potential application in new federations. Although the full set of information required by a potential user\linebreak
will go well beyond the SOM contents, providing easy access to characterizations of simulation based on\linebreak
reuse potential will enable users to decide more effectively whether to invest in further assessment of the\linebreak
simulations to their applications. There are certain circumstances, such as dynamically configured passive\linebreak
subscribers, where a federate’s SOM is based on the current FOM.\par
\vspace{23.42pt}
